01 · PROJECT OVERVIEW

Connecting print settings to real-world performance.

Objective

This project evaluates which FDM materials and print settings are most effective for mechanical performance and surface finish. The goal is to turn experimental measurements into practical choices for additive-manufacturing professionals and hobbyists.

02 · KEY EDA HIGHLIGHTS

Three views of the printing process.

The dataset covers eight 3D printing materials: PLA+, PLA, PLA phosphor, PLA-CF, PETG, PP, TPU, and ABS. Each were examined across three infill densities (20%, 50%, and 80%) and three print speeds (30, 50, and 70 mm/s). 03 · KEY FINDINGS

What the data suggests.

01

PETG led in strength.

PETG produced the highest mean peak load in the analysis, making it the strongest material by that measure.

02

TPU had the smoothest finish.

TPU had the lowest average roughness, indicating the best surface finish among the compared materials.

03

More infill meant more strength.

Peak load and peak stress showed a clear upward trend as infill density increased.

04

Speed created a tradeoff.

Increasing print speed reduced surface quality, but peak load and peak stress increased—opposite the initial mechanical-performance expectation.

Overall Conclusion

There is no single “best” filament or setting. The right choice depends on whether the project prioritizes strength, finish, flexibility, material use, or production speed. To make the right selections, it is important to review the material and print setting performance data rather than relying on the manufacturer's descriptions alone.

04 · PROPOSED A/B TEST

Would objective performance data improve filament selection?

In this hypothetical scenario, a 3D printer filament distribution company sells through its website. New customers are randomly assigned by account to Version A or Version B to test whether performance data helps them choose an appropriate filament.

Finding: Filament descriptions often claim strength, but measured performance varies substantially.

This experiment tests whether adding measured performance data to product descriptions helps customers choose suitable materials the first time. The assumption is that descriptions alone may leave customers without enough information, leading to wasted time, money, and material when a filament fails to meet their needs.

A · BASELINE

Description only

Customers browse filament using the standard written material description.

B · PROPOSED

Description + performance data

Customers see the same description plus strength, surface roughness, and flexibility data.

Prediction: We expect that adding measured performance data will lead to a reduction in errant filament purchases because customers will be able to more accurately compare materials before purchasing.
Participants

New customers who own 3D printers and purchase filament, randomly assigned by account.

Main measure

Returns by customers making a replacement filament purchase because their initial material was unsuitable.

Test period

Six months, with both versions running concurrently.

Additional Tracked Variable

Time spent choosing filament, to identify possible confusion or decision overload.

What improvement would justify adopting B?

The company would consider adopting Version B if there was a significant decrease in returns as it shows overall greater satisfaction from the customer and a better chance for future revenue. The success threshold would be set a 20% decrease in returns. This represents a meaningful reduction in wasted time, money, and material and could improve customer satisfaction and retention.

Could better choices make shopping worse?

The test would also monitor time spent choosing a filament. Version B could reduce unsuitable purchases while making the experience worse if the added data overwhelms customers or makes decisions take much longer. Increased selection time would prompt the company to investigate whether customers were making useful comparisons or struggling to understand the information.

How could the comparison be misleading?

Differences in customer experience: A group with more experienced users might make better choices regardless of the website version. To address this, the test would randomly assign customers, but we cannot make sure each group is even in terms of experience without vetting particpiants first.

Stopping when B looks better: Stopping before both groups have enough time to complete returns would produce incorrect data. To address this, the company would commit to the six-month testing period before starting and complete it before evaluating the final results. This would allow sufficient time to complete returns.

Where the findings might not apply: Highly experienced users will be successful in their material choice regardless of data most likely. The test will focus on new users but ones that come in with experience will limit the success of the test.

What should be checked before crediting B?

If there is a significant decrease in returns in version B, the company would verify a number of things. First, we would confirm that random assignment worked and there is an equal number of participants in both groups. If possibile we would do further research to verify that the groups had comparable printing experience.

How would the company decide what comes next?

Adopt B: Selection-related replacement purchases fall by at least 20% relative to A, there are no unrelated reasons for this, and selection time remains acceptable.

Retain A: B produces little practical improvement or makes the purchasing experience substantially worse through increased purchase times.

Revise B and test again: Performance data improves material choices but overwhelms customers. The company would simplify the presentation or provide clearer comparisons before running another test.

How this decision connects to the project's central argument: descriptions alone may not provide enough information for suitable filament selection. If B improves choices without creating excessive difficulty, it would support including objective performance data for the customers studied.
